Our matcha

What's in the bowl

You don't need to know any of this to enjoy a cup. But if you want to, here's what we pour and why. Placeholder detail for the demo.

The cultivar

We work mainly with Okumidori — a shade-grown cultivar known for a deep green colour and low bitterness even as a thin whisk. Single-origin from Uji, Kyoto, rather than a house blend, so the character is consistent bowl to bowl.

The grades

  • Ceremonial — the youngest leaves, stone-ground fine. For usucha and koicha, drunk on its own.
  • Culinary — still good matcha, a touch more robust. This goes in the lattes and iced drinks, where milk and ice would flatten the ceremonial anyway.

Stone-ground, kept cold

Matcha is milled slowly on granite — a kilo takes hours — because heat and speed dull the flavour. We buy in small amounts, store it sealed and cold, and open a new tin often rather than hold a big one. Oxidised matcha turns hay-coloured and bitter; you shouldn't ever get that here.

Hojicha

Our hojicha is a medium roast of stems and leaf from Kyoto — roasted enough to be toasty and caramel-sweet, not so much that it tastes of ash. Low caffeine, which is why it's the afternoon pour.
