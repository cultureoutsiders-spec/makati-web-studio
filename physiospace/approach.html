The approach

One session, three ways in

Most clinics do one of these well. We built the room around doing all three in sequence, because that's the order the body seems to want.

Release what's guarding

When something hurts, the tissue around it tightens to protect it. Hands-on manual therapy — joint mobilisation, soft-tissue and fascial work — takes that guard down so the area can move again. This is the part you feel change during the session.

Retrain what overloaded it

Pain that keeps coming back is usually a movement pattern, not a spot. We use corrective and yoga-based movement to rebuild the pattern that failed — a few precise, repeatable things rather than a long sheet you won't do.

Settle what's holding it

A nervous system stuck in “on” keeps muscles braced and pain loud. Breathwork and sound — singing bowls, guided breathing — down-regulate that state so the work you just did gets filed as safe. It's the least dramatic part of the session and often why the relief lasts.

Who it suits

Persistent neck, back or shoulder pain. Recurring injuries that “heal” and return. Tension that lives in the body under stress. People who've done straight physio and want something that also addresses why it keeps happening. It is not emergency care — if you've had a sudden serious injury, see a doctor first.
