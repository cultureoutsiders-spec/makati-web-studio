The longer story

It started as lunch for a meditation class

No business plan, no investor deck. Just a group of people in Makati cooking plant-based meals for each other, and the realisation that they were good enough to sell.

2007Shared lunches

A small meditation community in Makati started cooking together — vegetarian, then fully vegan — because it fit how they wanted to live. The food got a reputation before the kitchen had a name.

the 2010sA room of our own

Demand outgrew a home kitchen. Greenery Kitchen took a modest space on E. Pascua Street in Barangay Kasilawan — a few tables, an open kitchen, the same cooks. The menu stayed Filipino and stayed meat-free: sisig, kaldereta, lechon paksiw, pancit, all built from plants.

today4.7 stars and a meal plan

Now it’s a daily à la carte kitchen with a loyal following — 4.7★ across 146 Google reviews — plus the 5-Day Bento plan that feeds regulars all week, and delivery across Makati, Mandaluyong, San Juan, BGC, Pasay and Taft.

the methodFrom scratch, by hand

  1. Plant-based, no exceptions

    No meat, no seafood, no fish sauce. Umami comes from mushrooms, soy, fermented things and time.

  2. Filipino first

    We’re not reinventing the dishes — we’re cooking the ones people grew up with, without the animal parts.

  3. Cooked daily

    The menu rotates because it’s cooked fresh each day, not batch-frozen and reheated.
