What are you wondering about?

Speech & language therapy

Understanding, talking, and being understood

For children who are late to talk, hard to understand, stutter, have trouble following instructions, or struggle to hold a back-and-forth conversation. Also feeding and swallowing concerns in younger children.

Signs it might help

  • Few or no words by 18–24 months
  • Speech that's unclear to people outside the family past age 3
  • Doesn't respond to their name or follow simple directions
  • Frustration or meltdowns around communicating

What a session looks like

45 minutes, mostly play — the therapist builds language into games the child already enjoys, and coaches you on what to carry over at home. Progress is reviewed with you every few weeks.

Occupational therapy

The everyday skills of being a kid

For children who find handwriting, dressing, using utensils, or fine-motor tasks hard; who are very sensitive to sound, textures or movement (or seek it out constantly); or who struggle with attention, transitions and self-regulation.

Signs it might help

  • Avoids or tires quickly at drawing, cutting, buttons, laces
  • Strong reactions to noise, tags, food textures, or messy play
  • Always moving, crashing, or seeking pressure
  • Hard time settling, switching tasks, or coping with “no”

What a session looks like

A mix of a sensory “warm-up”, a fine-motor or self-care goal, and a regulation strategy — then a short handover so the same approach works at home and school.

Request an assessment

Physical therapy

Movement, strength, and balance

For children with delayed milestones (rolling, sitting, walking), low or high muscle tone, poor coordination or balance, toe-walking, or recovery after an injury or surgery.

Signs it might help

  • Milestones noticeably behind (not walking by ~18 months)
  • Frequent falls, clumsiness, or fear of playground equipment
  • Walks on toes most of the time past age 2
  • Tires much faster than peers in active play

What a session looks like

Play-based movement work targeting one or two goals, with a home programme of a few short exercises you can fit into the day.

SPED tutorial

Learning support, at the child's pace

One-on-one tutorial for children with additional learning needs — pre-academic readiness, reading and writing, numeracy, and school-work support, using a structured, individualised plan.

Signs it might help

  • Falling behind class work and losing confidence
  • Reading, writing or number skills not clicking with the usual methods
  • Needs a lot of repetition and structure to hold new skills
  • School has recommended additional support

What a session looks like

A short, focused block on one or two targets, broken into small steps with lots of practice, and a note to you (and, with permission, the school) on what was covered.

A rough guide

What's typical, by age

Every child is different and these are broad ranges, not deadlines. If something here rings true, an assessment will tell you more than a checklist can.

12–18 mo

First words, points to ask for things, walks, copies simple actions.

2 years

Two-word phrases, follows simple instructions, runs, scribbles, uses a spoon.

3 years

Short sentences, mostly understood by others, pedals a trike, draws a circle.

4–5 years

Tells a short story, holds a pencil, dresses with little help, hops, takes turns.

School age

Reads and writes at grade level, sits for lessons, manages friendships and routines.

Common questions

Good to know

How young is too young?

It isn't. Early support tends to work faster. We assess children from about age one — sometimes the outcome is “keep watching, come back in three months,” and that's a useful answer too.

Do you take HMO or insurance?

Some sessions can be covered depending on your provider and plan. Tell us your HMO when you book and we'll confirm what's covered and what needs a letter of authorisation. Accepted providers to be confirmed by the centre.

How much does it cost?

Assessment and per-session fees are quoted before you commit, and vary by discipline and session length. Current fees to be confirmed by the centre.

Can my child see more than one therapist?

Yes — many do. Because everyone works from the same records and talks to each other, the plan stays joined-up rather than four separate plans.

Do you coordinate with schools?

With your permission, yes — we can share a summary and recommendations, and join a meeting if that helps.
